To verify, on PCs to ping each other?

YES—pinging between your PCs is the ultimate end-to-end test!
When a PC in one department can successfully ping a PC in another department (or across the campus on a different switch), it proves that your Layer 2 trunks, HSRP Virtual Gateways (.254), ROAS subinterfaces, and OSPF routing tables are all working together seamlessly.
Here is your 4-Step PC Verification Matrix to test every layer of your campus network:
1. Inter-VLAN Routing Test (Different Departments)
This tests whether your PCs can send traffic to their HSRP default gateway (.254) and have R1 or R2 route the packet between VLANs.
From: An Engineering PC in VLAN 10 (192.168.10.x)  
To: A Sales PC in VLAN 20 (192.168.20.x) or Admin PC in VLAN 30 (192.168.30.x)  
Command (in PC Desktop $\rightarrow$ Command Prompt):
>>>
ping 192.168.20.x
<<<

(Replace .x with the actual DHCP IP address of the destination PC!)

What it proves: Your ROAS subinterfaces (gig0/0/1.10, .20, .30) and HSRP virtual IPs are actively routing packets between subnets.  

2. Cross-Campus Test (Same Department, Different Switch)
This tests whether your dot1Q trunks across SW1 and SW5 are properly forwarding tagged VLAN frames across the campus fabric without leaking or blocking them.  
From: Eng1 on SW2 (fa0/21, VLAN 10)  
To: Eng3 on SW6 (fa0/21, VLAN 10)  
Command:
>>>
ping 192.168.10.x
<<<

What it proves: Your Spanning Tree topology and inter-switch trunks (SW2 $\leftrightarrow$ SW1/SW5 $\leftrightarrow$ SW6) are forwarding VLAN 10 end-to-end.  

3. North-South WAN Test (Internet Reachability)
This tests whether your PCs can escape the campus network and reach the simulated Internet through your core routers and ISP.  
From: Any PC (Eng, Sales, or Admin)
To: The ISP Gateway (203.0.113.1 or 203.0.114.1) or simulated Internet DNS (8.8.8.8)  
Command:
>>>
ping 203.0.113.1
<<<

What it proves: Your OSPF default route injection (default-information originate), NAT/WAN routing, and ISP ECMP return routes are 100% functional.  

4. The Ultimate Redundancy Test (Live Failover)
Now for the test that separates a basic lab from an enterprise network: testing your HSRP and OSPF failover live while traffic is running.

4.1 Open Eng1 ---> Desktop ---> Command Prompt.
4.2 Start a continuous ping to a PC in another VLAN or to the ISP:
>>>
ping -t 203.0.113.1
<<<

4.3 Leave that window running, go into R1, and administratively shut down its WAN link or LAN trunk:

>>>
R1# configure terminal
R1(config)# interface gig0/0/0
R1(config-if)# shutdown
<<<

4.4 Look back at your PC prompt:

You will typically see 1 or 2 Request timed out messages while OSPF and HSRP detect the link drop.

Then, the pings will automatically turn solid green again as R2 takes over the traffic!



A Quick Reminder on the "First Ping Drop" Rule
Whenever you ping a new IP address for the very first time in Packet Tracer, remember to press Alt + D a few times first!

Even then, the very first ping will often time out (4/5 or 80% success) while the PC sends an ARP request to learn the destination MAC address. Just run the ping command a second time, and you should see 100% (4/4) success every time!
